HOW A SWAP WORKS

Four stages, and a difference settled in cash.

01

Independent valuation

Each property is valued on its own merits by a valuer instructed for that property. Neither owner values their own asset, and neither valuation is shown to the other side before both are complete.

02

Matching

Both values are expressed in one currency at a rate fixed on an agreed date, so a house in Lagos and a flat in London can be compared without argument about which day the rate came from.

03

Escrow

Properties rarely match to the naira. The difference is paid into escrow by the owner of the lesser asset and released only when both transfers complete.

04

Title transfer

Each side transfers under the law that governs its own property, with its own conveyancer. Girard holds the timetable and neither transfer completes without the other.

QUESTIONS
Is a match guaranteed?

No. A swap needs a counterparty who wants your property as much as you want theirs. Registering a property puts it in front of that search. It does not create the other side of it, and we will not pretend otherwise while you wait.

What does it cost?

Valuation, legal work and any transfer taxes are payable on each side as they would be on a sale. Girard charges a transaction fee for running the exchange, agreed in writing before the process starts. There is no fee for registering a property or for a search that finds nothing.

Who holds the escrow?

A licensed third party under written escrow terms both owners sign. Girard does not hold the money, and the release condition is the completion of both transfers rather than anybody's satisfaction.

What if one side cannot complete?

Neither transfer completes. The escrow returns to the party who paid it in, less costs already incurred, on the terms agreed at the start. The failure mode is written down before anybody signs, which is the part most informal arrangements skip.

Can a swap cross borders?

Yes, and that is the case it was built for. Each property transfers under its own law with its own conveyancer. What Girard adds is the common valuation, the fixed rate, the escrow and one timetable across both jurisdictions.

Does the property have to be mortgage-free?

Not necessarily, but any charge on the title has to be dealt with before transfer, and the lender has to agree. A property with an unresolved charge cannot be exchanged, the same as it could not be sold.

Where this stands today.

The swap service is open for registrations and the process above is the process we will run. It is a young service, so if you are the first through it you will be dealing with people who will tell you so rather than implying a queue that is not there.
